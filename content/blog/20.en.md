Our agency was approached by a man from one of Russia’s regions with an unusual request: to find his wife’s biological mother. His wife had turned forty, and only recently had she learned that she had been adopted. She found out by chance: shortly before her adoptive mother’s death, she overheard her talking with a friend. Now she wanted to know the truth about her origins.

### Forty Years Later

The task was difficult: the events were forty years old, and much official data is kept for only ten years before being destroyed. There was almost no starting information: the mother’s name, her approximate age and the city where she may have lived. But we took on the case: the truth matters in itself, whatever it turns out to be.

### Step by Step

The detectives started with the district where the mother was presumably from and gradually reconstructed the picture of her life up to the girl’s adoption. We established the biological mother’s exact details and found witnesses — neighbors who remembered the girl until she was five or six. That became the key to the case.

### Hard News

Sadly, the mother could not be found alive: she had died after being hit by a truck. Telling our client this was not easy. To prepare her, the detectives held a delicate conversation and told her in advance about the possible outcomes — given the mother’s age and the long years without any news of her. A person looking for their mother has the right to know the truth — and also the right to be prepared for it.

> The truth about the past can be bitter — but even a bitter truth can bring a family back together.

### New Hope

The story did not end there. We found out that our client’s wife had a half-brother living in another city. We contacted him — and brother and sister were finally able to meet after forty years apart. A light appeared in a hard story.

### If You Have Learned That You Were Adopted

Such news rarely comes at the right time: often a person finds out by chance, already as an adult, when there is almost no one left to ask. What remains are fragments — a name, a city, an approximate age. But even with these a search is possible: in this story they were enough to identify the mother and find the brother.

If you have questions about your own past or the fate of your loved ones, they are worth asking even when there is almost no information: a name, an approximate age and a city are sometimes enough to begin a search.
