Two years ago a Porsche Cayenne was stolen in Novosibirsk. For its owner it was not just an expensive car but a symbol of the success he had worked toward for many years. Searches through the police and private services found nothing: report after report — and the car seemed to have vanished into thin air.

Over two years, hope almost faded. The owner understood that the chances of finding the car after so much time were negligible. The insurance payout made up neither for the loss nor for what the car had meant to him. And yet he decided to make one more attempt and came to our agency.

### Where We Started

We understood how hard it is to find a stolen car two years later. The detectives began by gathering everything: they questioned the owner in detail, reconstructed his route on the day of the theft and studied the documents. They checked databases of stolen cars, possible criminal connections and the schemes by which expensive cars disappear into the “gray” market. In such cases every detail matters: a lead often hides where no one has looked.

### A Trail Beyond the Region

A month later the first results came in: one of our analysts found traces leading outside the Novosibirsk region. The most active phase began. We worked in several regions, with local agencies and with specialists in the resale of expensive cars. The key turned out to be a chain of documents forged for the sale. It led us to people who were apparently involved in the theft and resale.

### The Car Is Found

Two months after the search began, the car was found. It was in a small town in another region of Russia, where someone was trying to sell it with forged documents. Together with the local law enforcement agencies, our detectives returned the car to its rightful owner — intact and in almost the same condition as when it was stolen.

> Even when hope seems lost, there is still a chance to recover what was stolen.

### If Your Property Has Been Stolen

This story shows that even years later, when all deadlines seem to have passed, it is possible to recover what was lost. But the sooner a search begins, the better the chances of success. If your car has been stolen or other valuable property has gone missing, don’t delay — time works for those who took it.

Right after a theft, it is important not only to report it to the police but also to write down everything you remember: where and when the car was last parked, who showed interest in it, whether there were any strange calls. Such details often lead to a trail later. And don’t trust people who promise to “get your car back” for money without any guarantees: fraudsters are happy to profit from someone else’s misfortune.
