Our agency was approached by a man looking for his stepbrother. As far as he knew, the brother had lived in Ukraine until 1985, after which contact between them was lost. Where he was, whether he was even alive — our client did not know, and this question had troubled him for many years.

### The Difficulty of Time

Finding a person after so many years is not easy. There were no electronic databases back then, and most information, especially archival records, is not kept for more than ten years. Documents are destroyed or inaccessible, and a person’s path has to be pieced together bit by bit. Over such a span everything changes: surnames, addresses, cities, sometimes even countries. People who might remember something move away or pass away. That is why in such cases it is not only the paperwork that matters, but also conversations with people who might have known the family.

Our detectives took on the case. There were trips to different regions and a great many archival documents and other sources that might shed light on the brother’s fate.

### The Brother Is Found

After several weeks of intensive work, we found him. The brother was living with his family in a Ukrainian city and was already raising grandchildren. He had turned 71, and over the years he had built his life. When our detective told him that his stepbrother was looking for him, he was overjoyed. Learning that you are remembered and sought after so many years is a powerful experience even for someone who has seen a lot.

> Family ties have no expiration date — you can find your own people even after a whole lifetime.

### A Meeting as Soon as Possible

The brother wanted to meet as soon as possible. Given their age and how long the brothers had waited for this meeting, we did our best to arrange it as quickly as possible: at that age, every month of waiting counts. Both sides warmly thanked us for restoring the connection.

### Don’t Lose Hope

For us, stories like this are the best reward. However many years have passed, it is never too late to look for family. If you have lost touch with a brother, a sister or someone else close to you, start with what you remember: the city, the years, the names of mutual acquaintances. Old letters, photos, postcards with a return address — all of this can be useful too. Even a little can be the start of a search.
