Our agency was approached by the mother of a teenager. Her son had changed sharply and unpredictably: outbursts of aggression, rudeness to his parents and neighbors, skipping school, coming home late smelling of tobacco and sometimes alcohol. When his parents tried to talk to him, they heard “Stay out of my life!” — or even swearing.

Such stories are not rare in our practice. The sooner parents notice the warning signs, the better the chance of helping in time.

### Who Is Around Their Son

The detectives began with the description of the teenager his parents gave us and used it to draw up a plan: to establish his circle and the places he goes. Surveillance showed that the son had fallen under the influence of older “friends”. They got him used to cigarettes and alcohol and pushed him toward far more dangerous steps.

### One Step from Crime

The older friends were egging the teenager on to take part in robberies and other crimes. All of this was presented as “friendship” and “adult life”, and he himself did not understand where it could lead. Such groups often lure young people with the promise of freedom, but in reality they simply use them.

Our detectives identified all the members of the group, their meeting places and how they drew in newcomers. We passed this information, together with the evidence, to the law enforcement agencies, and the group’s activity was stopped at an early stage.

> Timely intervention is what separates a warning sign from a tragedy.

### Back to a Normal Life

The teenager was saved in time. His parents received all the materials and understood what the timely intervention had protected them from. Their son broke free from the group’s influence and returned to a normal life and his studies.

### The First Signs

Be on your guard if your teenager:

- has abruptly changed his circle of friends and doesn’t want to talk about the new ones;
- has become aggressive and rude and no longer listens to his parents;
- skips school and comes home late;
- comes home smelling of tobacco or alcohol;
- brings home things or money he cannot explain — or, on the contrary, money has started disappearing from home.

Each of these signs is a reason not to wait for things to pass on their own. A problem that looks like ordinary adolescence may turn out to be the beginning of real trouble. It is better to find out who your child spends time with before the police tell you.
