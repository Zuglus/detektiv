Want to buy an apartment or a house, invest in housing at the construction stage, make an exchange or rent an expensive office? Before handing over money, check the property’s legal status. We will find out whether the owner’s documents are in order, whether the property is mortgaged to a bank, and whether there is any other hidden history behind it.

### Why Real Estate Deals Are Risky

Scams involving the sale of apartments, houses, offices and land are a problem not only in Russia. The deals are expensive, there are many legal subtleties, and fraudsters who work with uninformed people have plenty of loopholes. The most common schemes:

- property that is mortgaged to a bank or under seizure is sold;
- the real owner does not know about the sale or is against it;
- a developer invites you to invest in building a house, but legally does not exist or has problems with the law.

Checking a property’s legal status is an essential step before buying or renting. In practice, however, many people buying a resale apartment take a standard form from the internet and take on faith the clause saying the property is not mortgaged or seized.

> A property’s legal status should never be taken on faith — it must be checked.

### Signs of a Dubious Deal

Be on your guard if:

- the price is well below market, and the seller is rushing the deal;
- the apartment is being sold under a power of attorney, and the owner never appears;
- the property has changed owners several times in a short period;
- the seller shows only copies of the documents.

None of these signs on its own means fraud. But together they are a reason to check the deal before handing over money.
