Our agency was approached by a man who had traveled on the Anapa–Moscow train. In the next compartment was a woman he took a liking to. They got acquainted on the way but never exchanged contacts.

Time passed, but the encounter would not leave his mind. He was single, and as far as he knew, so was she — and he decided to renew the acquaintance. Only one thing was missing: a way to get in touch with her.

### Found from a Single Journey

It is not an easy task: usually there is no phone number, no address, and often not even a surname — only the shared journey. But even a single trip leaves traces. Everything you can remember helps: the date and number of the train, the carriage and seats, where the person was going, what they said about themselves — their work, family, hometown. A detail mentioned in passing sometimes turns out to be the key.

Our client had little to go on: the train, the next compartment, and what he remembered from their conversations on the way. Our detectives did painstaking work and established the woman’s contact details.

### The Choice Was Hers

With our client’s agreement, we passed his contact details on to the woman and asked her to get in touch — rather than the other way around. That way the decision remained hers: whether to call or not was up to her. A person who has been found has the right to decide for themselves whether they want the acquaintance to continue.

The woman was delighted that we had found her and contacted him right away. The acquaintance continued on equal terms: not because she had been tracked down, but because she wanted it herself. Both of them thanked us for our work. It is a real pleasure to watch from the sidelines as such unions come about.

> A chance meeting can become more than chance — if you don’t let it slip away a second time.

### Finding a Fellow Traveler

People often come to us with similar requests — to find a fellow traveler. A journey brings people together only briefly: a few hours or days side by side, and then everyone goes their own way. On the road, people open up more easily than in everyday life — perhaps because they don’t expect to meet again. Perhaps that is why such encounters stay with you.

Many people first try searching on their own — on social media, through “looking for you” groups. Sometimes it works, but more often the search runs up against the fact that almost nothing is known about the person. And a public post with a photo can put the person being sought in an awkward position.

In the same way, it is possible to find someone you met in any chance encounter: on vacation, on a business trip, at a friend’s place.
