Our agency’s private detectives gather information on criminal and civil cases — and more. We help find evidence and witnesses and clarify the picture in a divorce or the division of a business. Experience in law enforcement allows us to obtain information of any complexity — lawfully and quickly.

### Why You Need a Detective in a Court Case

People come to us to:

1. speed up the proceedings in a case;
2. obtain additional evidence and details;
3. confirm or check an alibi and other facts;
4. properly put together a package of documents for court.

An investigator handles dozens of cases, and a court evaluates only what is presented to it. A detective works on one case — yours — and looks for what could change its outcome. This is especially important when time is short: court deadlines don’t wait, and evidence is lost over time.

### Civil Cases

A detective is needed not only in criminal cases. In civil disputes — divorce and the division of property, debt recovery, inheritance disputes, conflicts between co-owners of a business — the outcome is often decided by facts that one side tries to hide: where the defendant really lives, what property he has, who can confirm important circumstances. Gathering such information lawfully and in time is our job.

### What We Do

Our detectives come from law enforcement and know how an investigation works from the inside. We will draw up an official request for information on a criminal or civil case, prepare documents for the investigation and the court, and help you organize your work with law enforcement agencies.

By turning to us, you get professional support, an outside view and a reliable way to gather the information you need.

> Information carries weight when it is gathered lawfully and properly documented.

### Lawfulness First

Information helps a case only when it has been obtained lawfully: otherwise the court simply will not accept it. That is why we work strictly within the law on private detective activity and document what we gather so that it can be used in court. Before promising anything, we tell you honestly what can be obtained by lawful means and what cannot.

Ask your questions — a private detective will answer them.
