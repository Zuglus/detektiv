Our agency was approached by a businessman from a well-known Moscow firm. He had begun to doubt the honesty of his close friend and business partner. Their story began back at university: together they came up with the idea for their first company and founded it, and over time they built it up to a turnover of many millions.

### The Firm Fades, the Partner Thrives

Lately everything had gone wrong. Profits were falling rapidly, employees were leaving without explanation, and the business was fading before his eyes. Meanwhile the partner was living better and better: buying expensive cars and luxury real estate, and apparently not worried at all about the firm’s affairs.

### The Last Straw

Even earlier, a major contract with one of the firm’s key partners had fallen through for no apparent reason. The last straw was a board meeting held to discuss a way out of the crisis: the partner stayed indifferent and did not look for solutions. That is when our client came to us.

### A Parallel Company

The detectives started with the partner’s financial flows and business activity — and quickly confirmed that the suspicions were justified. The partner had secretly opened an identical company and registered it in the name of one of his parents. He lured part of the joint firm’s staff there and took the client base.

The remaining employees were also part of the scheme. They were still on the books at our client’s firm and received their salaries and bonuses there, but they worked for the new company: clients who contacted the joint firm were sent to the partner.

> The business did not collapse on its own — a close friend was destroying it from within.

### Seeing It With His Own Eyes

Even with the facts in hand, our client did not want to believe in the betrayal. He went to the new firm to see for himself — and found his partner’s parents in the director’s chair, surrounded by the lured employees, who had never expected to run into their former employer. A conversation with his “friend” put everything in its place: the reason was ordinary greed.

### Warning Signs

Betrayal in business is rarely sudden — the signs are usually visible long before the outcome:

- profits fall although the market and demand have not changed;
- employees and clients leave, and for no clear reason;
- deals that seemed settled fall through;
- the partner loses interest in the joint business while his personal wealth grows.

Each sign can be explained on its own. But when they add up, it is time to check rather than wait.

We handed the evidence we had gathered to our client. This case shows that even the closest people in business are worth checking — especially when the state of the firm and the partner’s prosperity begin to diverge.
